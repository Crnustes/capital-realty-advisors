Frequently Asked Questions

What is the importance of the December 31, 2026 deadline?+

The December 31, 2026 deadline is the statutory cutoff to submit an Act 60 decree application to the DDEC under Track 1, which secures a 0% tax rate on personal passive income and federal capital gains through 2035.

What changes if I apply after January 1, 2027?+

Applying after January 1, 2027 places your Act 60 decree under Track 2 rules, which increases the personal passive income tax rate from 0% to 4% through 2050 and introduces additional eligibility restrictions.

Does Capital Realty Advisors of Puerto Rico, LLC handle annual tax filings directly?+

Capital Realty Advisors of Puerto Rico, LLC does not file tax returns directly; instead, the firm manages your overall compliance strategy by tracking residency days, donation obligations and annual fillings, coordinated with specialized CPAs who execute the filings.

How long does the legal preparation process take for an Act 60 decree application?+

The full process has two phases. Legal preparation with your attorney — gathering documents, structuring your application, and building your compliance package — typically takes 4 to 8 weeks. Once submitted, DDEC processing takes an additional 60 to 120 days. In total, expect 6 to 12 months from start to approved decree in hand.

The critical detail for the December 31, 2026 deadline: What matters is the timestamp of your application filing — not the date DDEC approves it. This means you need to start the legal preparation process now, not in November. Attorneys with active Act 60 practices are already at capacity heading into year-end.

Is there a minimum purchase price for the Act 60 real estate requirement?+

There is no statutory minimum purchase price for the Act 60 real estate requirement, though typical advisory clients acquire luxury homes ranging from $500,000 to over $3,000,000.

Are Act 60 property acquisitions restricted to specific areas in Puerto Rico?+

Act 60 property acquisitions are not restricted to specific geographic areas in Puerto Rico, allowing decree holders to purchase qualified residential property anywhere on the island, including Dorado, San Juan, Condado, Isla Verde, Humacao, and Rincón.

What is involved in annual act 60 compliance?+

Annual Act 60 compliance involves maintaining physical presence records to prove 183 days of residency, completing a mandatory $10,000 annual charitable donation to qualified local non-profits, and filing an annual report with the DDEC.

Can I maintain my existing business entities in the mainland after moving under Act 60?+

Maintaining existing business entities in the mainland is permitted after moving under Act 60, provided your corporate entities are properly structured by specialized CPAs to comply with federal income-sourcing regulations.

An Act 60 advisory firm differs from a standard real estate agent by delivering an integrated consultancy framework—coordinating legal decree filings, managing luxury real estate acquisitions, and providing ongoing annual compliance oversight.
